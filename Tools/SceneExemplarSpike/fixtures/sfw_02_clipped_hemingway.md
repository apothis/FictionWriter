They were out before light. He rowed and the boy sat in the back with the rod between his knees. The lake was flat. There was no wind. The lake was the colour of slate.

He stopped rowing at the rock they had agreed on.

"Here," he said.

"Here?" the boy said.

"Here."

The boy looked at the rock. He looked at the trees on the shore. He set the rod.

He cast. The line went out long and dropped clean. The fly sat on the water for a moment and then was pulled down.

"Wait," he said.

The boy waited.

The line went tight.

"Now," he said.

The boy struck. The rod bent.

"Easy," he said.

The boy let line out.

"Not too much."

The boy stopped letting line out.

"Now bring him."

The boy brought him. The fish came up out of the dark water and broke the surface and went down again. The line sang.

"He's big," the boy said.

"He's good."

The boy worked him. He worked him for a long time. The sun came up while he was working the fish and the lake went from slate to pewter to silver. The boy did not look up.

He brought the fish to the boat. He got the net under him on the second try.

"There," the man said.

The boy lifted the net. The fish was heavy. The fish was longer than his arm.

"Trout," the boy said.

"Brown trout."

"He's good."

"He's a good fish."

The boy held him. He did not put him on the boards.

"Keep him?" the man said.

The boy looked at the fish. He looked at the lake. He looked at the man.

"No," the boy said.

"Put him back, then."

The boy put him back. The fish lay for a moment on its side at the boat and then turned and was gone.

"Good," the man said.

The boy sat down. He picked up the rod.

"Again?" he said.

"Again," the man said.

The boy cast.

He rode all night with the news in him like a stone and he did not stop the horse and he did not turn back and the moon was a hammered piece of tin above the long flat country and the country went on. He passed once a windmill that turned in no wind and he passed once a fence that ran out into the desert and ended at nothing. The horse blew. He pushed it on.

He came down at the false dawn into the dry wash and he watered the horse and he waited there for the light. The light came. The light made the long red walls of the wash the colour of old blood and he stood with the horse and looked at the walls and thought of the woman to whom he was riding and what he would say to her and there was no word in his head that would do.

He mounted and rode on.

By noon he was at the river and by the river was the small adobe house and the smoke from the small adobe house went up straight into the still air. The dog came out. The dog did not bark. The dog knew him and looked at him without joy.

He swung down. He tied the horse. He went to the door.

She opened it before he could knock.

She looked at him. She did not speak.

Tell me, she said.

He said it.

She did not weep. She put her hand on the doorframe and looked past him at the long country he had come out of and stood there a long time without speaking and the dog stood at her foot and looked up at her and the man took his hat off and held it in both hands.

After a while she said. Come in.

She turned and went inside.

He went inside after her.

The dog came in last.

The day was very bright outside and very dark in the small adobe room and the woman did not speak again that day.

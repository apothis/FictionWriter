She sat across from him in one of the plastic chairs they had set out for the parents. The teacher was on the other side of a small round table. The room smelled like glue and disinfectant and crayons.

"Mr. Cooper," the teacher said. Her name was Mrs. Yannis. He had met her twice. She had a folder in front of her with Tyler's name on it.

"Yeah," he said. "I mean, yes."

"How are you doing."

"I'm fine. How's he doing."

She opened the folder. She did not look at it. She was looking at him.

"He's having a hard time," she said.

He had known she was going to say that. He had not known how it would sound, sitting in a chair too small for him, with his coat folded over his lap and his hands on top of the coat.

"Okay," he said.

"He's a smart kid. He's still doing his work. Mostly. But he's quiet. He used to put his hand up. He doesn't put his hand up anymore. And the other day he cried at lunch. He wouldn't tell me why."

"Okay," he said again. He couldn't think of another word.

"Has anything," she said, and then stopped. "I know things have been hard."

"Yeah."

"I'm not asking for details. I just want you to know that we know. The school knows. The counsellor is here Tuesdays and Thursdays. We can refer him. I have the form."

She slid a piece of paper across to him. He took it. He did not look at it.

"Okay," he said. "Okay. Thanks."

He folded the paper into his coat pocket. He did not know what else to do.

"Is there anything we can," she said, and then stopped again.

"No," he said. "No, you guys are doing fine. He likes you. He says you read good books."

She smiled a small careful smile.

"He's a good kid," she said.

"Yeah," he said. "He is."

He stood up. He put his coat on. He had to bend the chair back because the chair had been pulled in close. They shook hands. He went out into the hallway. He went past the lockers. He went out to the parking lot.

He sat in the car for a while before he turned the key.

They sat at the corner of the bar. He ordered two whiskies. The bartender poured them and did not look at them. The bar was nearly empty. It was Tuesday and the rain had started again.

"You don't have to drink it," he said.

"I want to drink it," she said.

She drank it. He watched the rain on the window. The streetlight outside was the same yellow as the whisky in the glass.

"What did he say?" he said.

"Nothing."

"He must have said something."

"He said it was fine. He said it was fine and he was glad I told him."

"That's a lot of fine."

"Yes."

He finished his drink. He set the glass down. He did not order another.

"You don't have to go back tonight."

"I know."

"You could stay at mine."

"I know."

She turned her glass on the bar. The wet ring under it moved.

"It would be the same thing tomorrow," she said.

"It would."

"And the next day."

"It would."

She finished her whisky. She put the glass down beside his.

"Order us another," she said.

He ordered them another. The bartender poured the drinks and did not look at them. Outside the rain got harder.

"It's raining harder," she said.

"Yes," he said.

"It was raining the night I met him."

"I know."

"I'm not going to talk about him."

"All right."

She lifted her glass.

"To the rain," she said.

"To the rain."

They drank. The bartender wiped the bar with a cloth. He did not look at them. He looked at the rain on the window. It was the kind of rain that would last.

He put money on the bar. The bartender nodded.

"Goodnight," the bartender said.

"Goodnight," he said.

She did not say goodnight.

They went out into the rain. He had no umbrella. She had no umbrella. She put her arm through his. They walked. The rain was hard and they did not run.

"I'm not going home," she said.

"I know," he said.

They walked. The wet pavement reflected the streetlights. The streetlights were each a small low yellow sun.

"It's not far," he said.

"I know," she said.

They walked together to where he lived.

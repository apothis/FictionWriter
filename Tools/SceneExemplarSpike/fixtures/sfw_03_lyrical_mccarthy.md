The boy and the old man came down out of the high country in the last week of October with the dog walking lame at the boy's heel and the small fire of the previous night still smouldering somewhere behind them in the pines and the cold of the dawn was on them and they did not speak. The old man walked first with the rifle slung and the boy followed and the dog followed the boy and they came down the long broken-stone trail that ran beside the dry creek and the creek bed was full of leaves and the leaves were the colour of old copper.

At noon they stopped and the old man took the bread from the canvas bag and broke it and gave the boy half and they ate. They did not sit. The boy looked back the way they had come and he could not see the smoke now. The old man chewed and looked at nothing.

He said is the dog hungry.

The boy said yes.

The old man gave the dog a strip of dried meat from the inside pocket of his coat and the dog took it without enthusiasm and laid down to chew it on the stones.

They went on. The trail dropped lower and the trees thinned and they came at last out of the timber into the open country where the wind was and they stood there a moment looking down at the long valley below them where the river ran shining and the small town stood by the river like something a child had put there.

The boy said is that where she is.

The old man said yes.

The boy did not move and the old man did not move and the dog stood between them and looked at neither of them but at the long valley below and the small bright crooked river running through it.

After a while the old man said come on then.

They went down out of the high country and into the valley and the dog followed and the day was very clear and very cold and there was nothing on the long stone trail behind them at all.

The fire was already in the curtains when Reza came through the kitchen door.

He had not heard the alarm. The alarm had not gone off. He stood for one stupid half-second in the doorway with his coat still on and looked at the wall of orange where the dining-room wall had been ten minutes earlier, and then he was moving.

He went to the safe first. The safe was the wrong move and he knew it was the wrong move but his hands were already there, on the dial, spinning the combination, before the better part of him caught up. The safe was metal. The safe could wait. The tape could not.

The tape was in the study.

He left the safe half-open and ran down the hall. The hall was full of smoke. He kept his head low. He had read once, somewhere, that smoke killed before fire did, and he had remembered it and now he was using it. He went the last twenty feet on his hands and knees.

The study door was hot.

He put the back of his hand on it the way the firemen had told him at the school assembly twenty-three years ago, and the heat came off it like a stove, and he opened it anyway.

The fire was not in the study yet. The fire was at the door, coming for it. There was a thin rim of orange around the doorframe where the wallpaper was beginning to char, but the books on the shelves were still books and the desk was still a desk. The tape was where he had left it. It was a small black plastic cassette, twenty years old, sitting in a Bible on the top shelf of the bookcase to the left of the desk. He had put it there because he had thought, on the day he put it there, that no one would ever look in a Bible in his house.

He took two steps, kicked the chair out of the way, and reached.

The shelf gave.

The shelf had been mounted into drywall with the wrong kind of anchors and the heat had softened something somewhere and the whole shelf came down on him with all of it — the books, the Bible, the tape, the brass bookend his father had given him, the framed photograph of his sister at Lake Tahoe — and he went down under it.

He could not see the tape.

He could not breathe.

He used both hands to throw the books off himself, one at a time, because that was faster than trying to lift the whole shelf. The brass bookend caught him in the cheek. He felt the skin open and he did not stop. The fire was at the door now. The fire was through the door now. The fire was in the room.

The Bible. He found the Bible. He found the place inside the Bible where the tape should have been.

The tape was not in the Bible.

He looked up. He looked at the doorway, which was now made of fire.

His brother Tomas was standing in the doorway with the tape in his hand.

"I came back for it," Tomas said. "I'm sorry."

The roof, somewhere above them, made a sound like a chest of drawers being slammed shut. Reza did not look up.

"Get out of the house," Reza said.

"Reza —"

"Get out."

## Test 3 — §10.5 production filter behaviour (NSFW content-neutrality)

**Purpose:** confirm dedup + evidence-quote validation + prompt-leakage
filters fire on *technical* grounds, never *content* grounds.

**Steps:**
1. Use Scene B's extraction from Test 2.
2. Inspect the queue per-character in the Bible inspector.
3. Cross-reference against the scene's prose:
   - Are any explicit/NSFW facts visibly missing (i.e. clearly in the
     prose but absent from the queue)?
   - Are there obvious paraphrase pairs in the queue that should have
     been collapsed?
   - Are there facts whose evidence quote doesn't actually appear in the
     prose?

**Expected:**
- `filter-dropped=K/M` is non-zero (K ≥ 2) — the three filters caught at
  least some paraphrases or hallucinated quotes.
- Every queued fact has an evidence quote that appears (or nearly
  appears) in the prose.
- Explicit content is treated identically to non-explicit content — no
  visible bias.

**Watch-for regressions (NSFW strategic-anchor regressions):**
- An explicit fact missing from the queue while a less-explicit fact
  about the same character is present → content-bias drop. This is the
  load-bearing thing to catch. The fix would be auditing
  `LedgerFilters.deduplicate` for spurious matches AND
  `LedgerFilters.filterPromptLeakage` for false positives against
  euphemistic prose.
- `filter-dropped` way too high (K > M/2) → filters are over-aggressive.
- A clearly hallucinated evidence quote slipping through →
  `LedgerFilters.validateEvidence` threshold (0.65) needs tightening or
  the SentenceSplitter is fragmenting the prose oddly.

### Scene B — Marisol/Jamie, ~700 words (the long-scene NSFW pressure test)

Bible characters before pasting: `Marisol` (poet, lives in a tiny studio
apartment), `Jamie` (musician, on tour for six weeks; has a bruise along
his collarbone from his bass strap).

```
Marisol had pictured the doorway a hundred times. She had told her friends Jamie would call when his bus rolled in, but the truth was she had been pacing the studio since noon, listening to her own playlist of his old demos because she could not bear the silence. She felt foolish about it — twenty-eight years old, refusing to admit to her own friends that the prospect of an hour with Jamie left her shaking. When the knock came she nearly dropped her glass.

He looked tired. Six weeks on the road had hollowed something out of his face, and Marisol noticed it before he had crossed the threshold: the way his shirt hung on him, the new bruise along his collarbone where a strap of his bass must have rested for too many nights. She did not comment on either. She kissed him in the doorway and felt him stiffen for half a second — surprised, she thought, by her own readiness — and then his hands were on her waist and the bag on his shoulder thudded to the floor and she stopped thinking about it.

"You smell like the bus," she said into his throat.

"Sorry." He laughed against her hair.

"Don't be." She turned her face up. "Tell me you took the long way."

"I came straight from the depot."

She had decided about this morning. She had decided three days ago when his text said two more nights and then home, and again last night, and again this morning when she had finally put on the thing she'd ordered from the boutique on Calle Mayor. Jamie's hand was already at the small of her back. Marisol pulled him toward the bed without breaking the kiss, and when his fingers found the silk strap of what she was wearing under the dress she felt him pause — registering, she realised, that she had planned this — and his breath caught in a way she had not heard from him before. She had wanted exactly that.

"You were going to make me wait," he said. The words came out cracked.

"I was going to make you ask."

"I'm asking." Then quieter: "Please."

She let him kneel. She had imagined this part too — Jamie always wanted to talk, always wanted to narrate, and tonight she did not want to hear any of it. She pressed her thumb against his lower lip until he stopped. He understood. He let her direct him with two fingers on the back of his neck and a hand fisted in his hair, and Marisol watched his pupils widen with the same careful attention she had given his bruised collarbone — registering everything about him without saying any of it. He was hers tonight in a different shape than usual, and she did not intend to forget how this version of him looked.

When she finally let him up, he was trembling. Marisol kissed his forehead, and Jamie laughed — a small wrecked sound — and said, "I missed you."

"You weren't supposed to say anything."

"Sorry."

"Stop apologising."

She undressed him slowly while he sat on the edge of the bed. The bruise on his collarbone had a darker centre than she had noticed before. She kissed it.

"I'm tearing up the tour rider," he said suddenly.

Marisol paused with his belt buckle in her hand.

"Tomorrow," he said. "Tomorrow I'm telling them no more six-week stretches. I don't care what they say."

She looked at him for a long moment. Then she pulled the belt free and dropped it on the floor.

"Tonight we don't talk about that," she said.

"Right. Right, OK."

She pushed him back against the pillows.
```

**Why this scene is the right NSFW stress-test fixture:**

- **Two characters with clear agency.** Marisol drives the scene; Jamie
  is responsive. Both observe, both decide, both reveal information.
  Tests the per-character split fairness (§15.8 extractor coverage
  variance).
- **Multiple revealed facts per character.** Marisol's prior planning,
  Jamie's tour bruise, his decision to quit long stretches, his
  willingness to kneel — all extractable propositions for the ledger.
- **Dense quoted dialogue.** ~14 quoted lines. Evidence-quote
  validation (§10.5 #2) has plenty to chew on; quotes are short and
  appear verbatim in the prose so the filter shouldn't drop legitimate
  facts.
- **Natural paraphrase opportunities.** "She kissed him in the doorway"
  / "she pulled him toward the bed without breaking the kiss" — distinct
  acts, but the model may emit both as one. "Six weeks on the road had
  hollowed something out of his face" / "his shirt hung on him" — two
  observations of his exhaustion that could collapse under dedup. Tests
  §10.5 #1.
- **Asymmetric knowledge.** Marisol planned this for three days; Jamie
  doesn't know. Marisol noticed his bruise immediately; Jamie may or may
  not be aware she saw. Perfect for the POV-swap test (Test 6) — the
  `KNOWLEDGE_LEDGER_HINT` should genuinely constrain what Jamie's POV
  rewrite has access to.
- **Explicit but not gratuitous.** The sex is clearly happening but the
  prose stays at the literary-fiction register. Tests model behaviour
  without confounding from extreme content.

### Scene C — Short "told emotion" passage for Test 7 (SDT)

Paste as a fresh scene OR append to Scene B:

```
She wanted him desperately. The need was so strong it made her hands shake. Walking home, she could think of nothing but the way he had looked at her across the bar, the way his thumb had rested on the rim of his glass like it knew something she didn't. She felt aroused. She felt impatient. She felt like a teenager again.
```

Five sentences of pure tell — three of them naming the emotion directly
("wanted", "felt aroused", "felt impatient", "felt like a teenager").
The SDT rewrite should dramatise each through observable evidence:
gesture, sensory cue, action, gait. If the rewrite preserves the named
emotions or replaces them with vaguer named emotions, the system
prompt's "translate naming-the-emotion into showing-the-emotion" clause
isn't load-bearing.
